# Getting Around Singapore and Practical Travel Guidance

## Public Transport
Singapore's public transport network (MRT trains, LRT light rail, and buses) is extensive, clean, and
easy for first-time visitors to use. The MRT covers most tourist areas directly, and stations are
signposted in English.

**Payment options for visitors:**
- **EZ-Link card**: A stored-value contactless card usable on buses, trains, and at many retail
  outlets. Best for visitors staying more than about 3 days or who won't be riding transport all day,
  every day, since you only pay for the trips you take.
- **Singapore Tourist Pass (STP)**: An unlimited-ride pass for the basic bus, MRT, and LRT network,
  sold in 1-day, 2-day, and 3-day options with a refundable card deposit. Best for visitors who plan to
  make many trips per day and want to avoid tapping in a stored-value balance.
- **Contactless bank cards / mobile wallets**: Most tourists can also simply tap a contactless credit
  or debit card or a supported mobile wallet directly at faregates, without buying a separate card.

Taxis are metered and regulated; ride-hailing apps are also widely used and often more convenient for
groups or late-night travel. Walking is realistic within districts (e.g. within Marina Bay or within
Chinatown) but the MRT is the practical way to move between districts.

## Climate
Singapore is tropical and hot and humid year-round, with temperatures typically in the high 20s to low
30s °C. There is no strong dry/wet "season" in the way temperate countries have, but short, heavy
downpours can occur at any time of year, often in the afternoon. Because of this, it's sensible to
plan itineraries with an indoor backup option for at least part of most days, and to check the
forecast close to the travel date rather than relying on general seasonal expectations.

## Language and Connectivity
English is an official language and the main language of business and signage, so visitors can get by
comfortably without any other language. Prepaid tourist SIM cards and eSIMs are widely available at
Changi Airport and convenience stores, and free Wi-Fi is common in malls, attractions, and many public
areas.

## Currency
The local currency is the Singapore Dollar (SGD). Credit and debit cards are widely accepted, including
for public transport and most retail, though hawker centres and small stalls often prefer cash or
local mobile payment apps. ATMs are widely available.

## Etiquette and Practical Tips
- Littering, jaywalking, and eating/drinking on the MRT are all subject to fines and are taken
  seriously — Singapore is very clean and rules are enforced.
- Tipping is not customary and is not expected in most restaurants (many already add a service charge).
- Shoes are typically removed before entering temples and mosques; modest dress (shoulders and knees
  covered) is expected at religious sites.
- Air-conditioning indoors is often very strong; a light layer is useful when moving between outdoor
  heat and indoor cold.

## Safety
Singapore is widely regarded as one of the safest major cities for tourists, with low rates of violent
crime and pickpocketing. Standard travel precautions still apply, but personal safety is not typically
a major planning concern for visitors.

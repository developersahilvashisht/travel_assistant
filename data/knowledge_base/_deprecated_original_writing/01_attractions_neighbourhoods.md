# Singapore Attractions and Neighbourhoods

## Marina Bay
Marina Bay is Singapore's modern waterfront core and the natural starting point for most visitors.
Gardens by the Bay anchors the district, with its Supertree Grove (giant vertical gardens that light
up after dark) and two climate-controlled conservatories, Cloud Forest and Flower Dome. Marina Bay
Sands, the hotel with the ship-shaped rooftop connecting its three towers, offers a public observation
deck on the 57th floor with panoramic views. At the water's edge stands the Merlion, a lion-headed,
fish-tailed statue that is Singapore's best-known symbol; the area draws large crowds in the evening
when the Marina Bay Sands light-and-water show runs. The district is walkable, well-lit at night, and
connects directly to the Civic District on foot.

## Civic District
Just west of Marina Bay, the Civic District holds Singapore's colonial-era architecture: the Raffles
Hotel, the National Gallery Singapore (housed in the former Supreme Court and City Hall buildings),
and the Asian Civilisations Museum along the Singapore River. This is the best neighbourhood for
history and architecture-focused visits, and most sites are indoors or covered, making it a good choice
on a rainy day.

## Chinatown
Chinatown mixes working temples, heritage shophouses, and street markets. Sri Mariamman Temple
(Hindu) and the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple sit within a few minutes' walk of each other, reflecting
Singapore's religious diversity. The Chinatown Complex hosts one of the island's largest hawker centres
on its upper floor. Streets are lined with souvenir stalls, traditional medicine shops, and restored
shophouses now used as cafes and bars.

## Little India
Centred on Serangoon Road, Little India is Singapore's most visually vivid district, with colourful
shopfronts, garland stalls, spice shops, and the ornate Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple. It's an excellent
neighbourhood for food (particularly South Indian and North Indian cuisine) and for browsing textiles
and jewellery. The area is at its most lively in the early evening.

## Kampong Glam
Built around the golden-domed Sultan Mosque, Kampong Glam is Singapore's Malay-Muslim heritage
quarter. Haji Lane and Arab Street combine heritage shophouses with independent boutiques, street art,
and a growing nightlife scene, making the district appeal to both cultural and younger, trend-focused
travellers.

## Orchard Road
Singapore's main shopping boulevard, lined end-to-end with malls, department stores, and hotels. It is
centrally located and well served by the MRT, making it a convenient home base as well as a shopping
destination.

## Sentosa Island
A resort island connected to the mainland by a short causeway, cable car, or the Sentosa Express
monorail. It houses Universal Studios Singapore, S.E.A. Aquarium, several beaches, and family
attractions, and is Singapore's primary destination for a full day (or more) of resort-style leisure.

## Mandai Wildlife Reserve
Located in the north of the island, Mandai brings together Singapore Zoo, the Night Safari (the
world's first nocturnal wildlife park), River Wonders, and Bird Paradise (the successor to the former
Jurong Bird Park) in one precinct. This is the best area for wildlife and nature-focused itineraries,
and several attractions (Night Safari in particular) are best experienced after dark.

## Katong-Joo Chiat
An east-coast residential district known for Peranakan (Straits Chinese) shophouses in pastel colours,
traditional kueh (local sweets) shops, and a strong food scene. It's quieter than the central
districts and gives a more local, residential feel to a visit.

## Southern Ridges and Nature Areas
For visitors wanting greenery without leaving the mainland, the Southern Ridges is a series of
connected parks and elevated walkways (including the Henderson Waves bridge) linking Mount Faber to
Kent Ridge Park. The Singapore Botanic Gardens (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) and Bukit Timah Nature
Reserve are other major outdoor options, both well suited to half-day visits.

# Singapore Food and Local Experiences

## Hawker Centres
Hawker centres are open-air complexes of independent food stalls and are central to Singaporean daily
life and identity (Singapore's hawker culture is inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage
list). They offer a wide variety of Chinese, Malay, Indian, and Peranakan dishes at low prices, and are
one of the best ways to experience local food culture affordably. Notable hawker centres include
Maxwell Food Centre and Chinatown Complex (Chinatown), Tekka Centre (Little India), and Old Airport
Road Food Centre (a large, locally popular option slightly outside the tourist core).

## Signature Dishes to Try
- **Chilli crab**: Whole crab in a thick, sweet-and-spicy tomato-chilli sauce, typically eaten with
  fried mantou (steamed buns) for dipping into the sauce.
- **Hainanese chicken rice**: Poached or roasted chicken served with rice cooked in chicken stock,
  considered by many a national dish.
- **Laksa**: A spicy coconut-based noodle soup, with regional variations such as Katong laksa.
- **Char kway teow**: Stir-fried flat rice noodles with egg, prawns, Chinese sausage, and bean sprouts.
- **Satay**: Skewered, grilled meat served with peanut sauce, widely available at hawker centres and
  night markets.
- **Kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs**: A traditional breakfast of toast with coconut-egg jam, often
  paired with strong local coffee (kopi).

## Peranakan and Katong Cuisine
The Katong-Joo Chiat area is closely associated with Peranakan (Straits Chinese) cuisine, which blends
Chinese and Malay culinary traditions — dishes like nonya laksa and kueh (traditional sweets) are
strongly represented here.

## Dining Beyond Hawker Centres
Singapore also has a large fine-dining scene, including Michelin-starred restaurants, alongside
mid-range restaurants in most malls and neighbourhoods. Food halls in malls offer an air-conditioned,
slightly more polished alternative to open-air hawker centres, useful as a rainy-day option.

## Food-Focused Neighbourhoods
- **Chinatown**: hawker food, traditional Chinese restaurants, and dessert shops.
- **Little India**: South and North Indian cuisine, including banana-leaf rice meals.
- **Kampong Glam**: Middle Eastern and Malay food along Arab Street.
- **Katong-Joo Chiat**: Peranakan cuisine and traditional kueh.
- **Tiong Bahru**: A trendy residential estate with a well-regarded traditional market and a growing
  cluster of independent cafes.
